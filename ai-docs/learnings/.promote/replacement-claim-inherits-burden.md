**Rule:** A sentence written to REPLACE a false general claim inherits that claim's full burden of
proof. Before writing a corrected "every / each / all", enumerate the members the new quantifier
ranges over and check the claim against each one by reading them. A quantifier that got smaller is
not a quantifier that was verified.

**Why:** Correcting an overbroad statement feels like the careful half of the work, so the
replacement is written with the confidence earned by having just caught an error — and is reviewed
less than the sentence it replaces. The result is a claim that is narrower, still false, and now
carries the authority of a fix. Worse, the replacement is frequently wired into something that
enforces it, so a wrong correction is promoted from prose to a rule others must satisfy.

**Signal:** The correction contains a universal quantifier, and the set it ranges over is larger than
the one the author had in mind while writing. Three shapes in particular: the correction names
examples ("as X and Y do") without checking Z; the correction is made in the same change that adds a
guard or check whose behaviour the claim now describes; or the corrected sentence describes a
mechanism the author just changed, so the claim was true minutes earlier and is stale rather than
wrong. Any of the three means enumerate before writing.
